Here's the framework, generalised so it works on any Hyderabad project rather than just these three. Each one came out of something we actually found.

## The desk screen — do this before you visit anything

**Pull the RERA filing first, always.** It costs nothing and it is the only document the builder is legally accountable for. Everything below flows from comparing it against the brochure.

**Compare the RERA saleable area against the brochure's marketed area, unit by unit.** If they match to the square foot, the builder is honest about size. If the brochure number is larger, calculate the multiple — we found one project selling at exactly 1.25× its registered figure, which turned a seemingly cheap flat into the most expensive of three.

**Check whether the brochure publishes carpet area at all**, and whether it shows the full chain from carpet through built-up to super built-up. A builder who publishes the whole breakdown is telling you something about how they'll behave later; a twelve-page brochure with no carpet figure anywhere is also telling you something.

**Read the "Do you have any Past Experience?" field and compare it to the brochure's project list.** We found a builder claiming 35 completed projects who declared none, and another claiming 7,100 apartments who declared one plotted layout. The filing is the version with legal consequences.

**Count the landowner-promoters.** One corporate owner is clean; eleven individuals on an area-share arrangement means a fragmented title chain and uncertainty about who signs your deed.

**Check which documents are uploaded and which say "Not Uploaded."** The four that matter most are the Occupancy Certificate, the advocate's land title search report, the sanctioned building plan, and the status of conveyance. Absences are questions, not disqualifications, but they're the right questions.

**Look at the land deduction line** — the field combining road widening, FTL of tanks and nala widening. Under 1% is a clean site; 10% means something significant is being taken, and you need the split in writing because FTL and nala are a very different problem from road widening.

**Check whether the 70% separate account and 30% transaction account are populated.** They were blank on all three we examined, which matters enormously for an under-construction purchase and barely at all for a completed one.

**Read the per-unit booking flags to compute true absorption**, and discount it for landowner allocations. Whole floors booked at once usually means area-share, not demand.

**Compare the block-level completion dates against the task percentages**, and check the date the filing was pulled. A project at 50% fittings is either badly late or comfortably on schedule depending entirely on how long it has left — I got this wrong once by assuming a snapshot was current.

## Area and price

**Compute price per square foot of carpet, and refuse to compare on anything else.** All-in price divided by RERA carpet area. It is the only figure that survives across builders, and typically none of the marketing gives it to you.

**Establish which area goes on the sale deed before paying an advance**, because that's what you pay stamp duty and registration on.

**Note that efficiency varies from about 57% to 71%** across projects that look similar. A 70% flat gives you roughly 17% more usable floor than a 60% flat at the same nominal size, which usually swamps the headline rate difference.

**Get the actual registered transaction rate** from the Telangana revenue data rather than the portal asking rate. In one locality the gap was ₹4,135 against ₹5,450 — about 24%.

## The building, judged for someone in their seventies

**Count the lifts and divide by the flats per core**, then ask whether there's a second shaft. We saw ratios from about 32 flats per lift to 84, and one project that appears to run a single lift per block. A single lift has no fallback.

**Time the longest lift wait at 8:30 on a weekday**, three trials, and record the worst. This one measurement outranks most of the brochure.

**Get the generator scope in kilowatts and ask specifically whether lifts and common lighting are on full backup.** Answers ranged from "100% per flat excluding A/C and geysers" to a sentence that described the electricity metering and nothing else.

**Ask the water question three ways**: is there a municipal connection, is there a borewell, and is there a monthly charge for either. We found a project whose brochure implied central treatment but whose owners report no municipal supply and a monthly borewell levy.

**Count the floors, and treat this as a senior-suitability variable in its own right.** At six floors the stairs are a real fallback; at thirteen they are marginal; at twenty-three they are not a fallback at all.

**Check the orientation mix.** Two projects had no north- or south-facing flat in the entire development, which no floor choice can fix and which determines afternoon heat for the life of the property.

**Compute density on net land, not gross**, and be alert to whether the RERA parcel covers all the blocks on the site. We saw 63, 78 and 193 units per acre, and one case where the real figure might be 107 rather than 78 depending on whether two unregistered blocks share the land.

**Divide the parking bays by the entitlement**, and ask whether other blocks or a commercial component share the same cellar. One project had two bays of slack across 504 flats.

**Walk the ambulance route to the tower base at 6pm** and count the turns and the parked cars.

**Look at bathroom dimensions on the floor plan.** A 6-foot by 4-foot bathroom cannot take a grab rail and an assisting person.

**Check for a puja room** if that matters to the household — one of three had one.

**Establish whether any commercial component sits inside the compound**, and if so, whether its parking, entrances and boom barriers are genuinely separate from residential.

## Location and daily life

**Time the drive to the airport at 3am and at 5pm** using Maps with departure time set, because flights from the US land before dawn and the difference between same-side and opposite-side of the city is an hour each way, every visit.

**Walk to the nearest station and time it**, and ask whether one of your parents could make that trip alone and safely.

**Check the HMRL corridor lists directly** rather than trusting a sales claim. One of our three localities was explicitly absent from all seven Phase II corridors; another appeared in two separate proposals.

**Time the walk to a grocery and a chemist at your parents' pace**, and ask the chemist whether they deliver and stock repeat prescriptions.

**Identify where the ambulance goes for a cardiac event at 2am** and how long it takes. Local 24-hour nursing homes are abundant everywhere; tertiary care is the actual constraint.

**Walk to the temple they would genuinely attend** and check the timings suit them. Temples inside the compound are worth noting separately.

**Visit on a weekday around 6:30pm and look at who is out walking.** Age-peer community shows up in ten minutes of observation and in no document.

**Search local reporting for waterlogging and power cuts** by locality name. Two of our three had documented histories, one including boat rescues.

## Using reviews as evidence

**Separate the sales-office listing from the builder's own listing.** The gap between them is the most diagnostic number available — one project showed 4.8 on its sales listing and 3.0 on its builder listing, where the owners actually post.

**Check what fraction of reviews contain text**, and read only those.

**Look at the monthly distribution for bursts.** Twelve five-star reviews in one month, half with no text, is a launch campaign. Twenty-six near-perfect reviews in two months praising the same staff member by name is a solicitation drive.

**Check reviewer credibility** — the share who are Local Guides and the median number of reviews per profile. Ours ranged from a median of 3 to a median of 20.

**Plot the rating trend by year.** An overall 4.6 concealed a collapse to 2.4 across the two most recent active months at one project.

**Read only the 1- and 2-star reviews, sorted newest first**, and search each listing for the words water, lift and maintenance.

**Weight multi-year resident accounts far above buyer accounts.** "I've lived here three years and maintenance answers" is worth more than fifty reviews praising the sales team.

## Risk and exit

**Ask whether the cellar has flooded and where the generator and lift machine rooms sit.** If they're in a cellar that takes water, a storm removes lifts and power simultaneously.

**Ask for the price dispersion in resales** over the last twelve months. One project showed listings from ₹70 lakh to ₹1.69 crore for comparable units, which is what an illiquid market looks like.

**Get the maintenance charge per square foot per month** and annualise it, because on a home with no rental income that is pure carry.

**Ask how many flats are occupied today.** A large, empty building has a maintenance-corpus problem that hits elderly residents first, through exactly the lifts and generators they depend on.

---

## How to sequence it

The first eight items are a free desk screen you can run on any project in about twenty minutes from the RERA portal alone, and they will eliminate most candidates before you spend a Saturday driving. Carpet rate and the area-basis question come next, because they reorder the shortlist. Everything physical — lifts, water, ambulance route, walking times — waits until you're standing there, and should all be done on the same morning across every remaining candidate so the comparison is fair.

One habit worth keeping: **when a brochure and a RERA filing disagree, the filing is the honest version, and the disagreement itself is information about the builder.** That single rule surfaced more in this exercise than any other test.

Want this as a screening checklist you can carry alongside the scorecard? It would work well as a short doc you fill in per candidate before deciding whether to visit.
